# Round 8 — full-3D free-scalar Ward complex on the staggered regulator

## Regulator and fixed data

Write lattice sites as integer triples and keep the lattice spacing `a`
explicit.  Define

\[
 G_i(x):=\frac{\phi(x+e_i)-\phi(x)}{a},\qquad
 \nabla_i^-G_i(x):=\frac{G_i(x)-G_i(x-e_i)}{a}.
\]

The Hamiltonian is `H_m = sum_x rho_x`; canonical variables are normalized per
cell, with the CCR stated below. A continuum field normalization or quantum
continuum-limit theorem is not part of this contract.

The free equations generated by the fixed Hamiltonian are

\[
 \dot\phi_x=\pi_x,\qquad
 \dot\pi_x=\sum_{\ell=1}^3\nabla_\ell^-G_\ell(x)-m^2\phi_x.
\]

Retain exactly the Round-7 density and link current:

\[
 \rho_x=\frac{\pi_x^2}{2}+\frac{m^2\phi_x^2}{2}
 +\frac14\sum_\ell\left(G_\ell(x)^2+G_\ell(x-e_\ell)^2\right),
\]

\[
 j_i(x)=-\frac12\left(\pi_x+\pi_{x+e_i}\right)G_i(x).
\]

Here `j_i(x)` is based at the link centre `x+e_i/2`.  No improvement of
`rho` or `j` is being made.

## Explicit symmetric stress

For a diagonal component, based at the site `x`, take

\[
\boxed{
 \tau_{ii}(x)=
 \frac{\pi_x^2}{2}-\frac{m^2\phi_x^2}{2}
 +\frac12G_i(x)G_i(x-e_i)
 -\frac14\sum_{\ell\ne i}
 \left(G_\ell(x)^2+G_\ell(x-e_\ell)^2\right).
}
\tag{R8.1}
\]

For `i != j`, based at the centre of the `(i,j)` plaquette with lower corner
`x`, take

\[
\boxed{
 \tau_{ij}(x)=\tau_{ji}(x)=
 \frac14\left(G_i(x)+G_i(x+e_j)\right)
          \left(G_j(x)+G_j(x+e_i)\right).
}
\tag{R8.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \tau_{ij}(x)=\frac{
  (\phi_{x+e_i+e_j}-\phi_x)^2
  -(\phi_{x+e_i}-\phi_{x+e_j})^2}{4a^2}.
\tag{R8.3}
\]

Equation (R8.3) makes the index symmetry and four-corner plaquette support
manifest.  The support is exactly finite:

- `rho`: one site and its six nearest neighbours in `phi`, onsite in `pi`;
- `j_i`: the two endpoints of its link;
- `tau_ii`: radius one about its site;
- `tau_ij`: the four corners of one elementary plaquette.

The staggered masks are therefore exactly the required ones: `rho` and
`tau_ii` have mask zero, `j_i` has mask `e_i`, and `tau_ij` has mask
`e_i xor e_j`.

## Theorem and proof

With the regulator derivatives appropriate to those masks,

\[
 (D_i\tau_{ii})(x)=\frac{\tau_{ii}(x+e_i)-\tau_{ii}(x)}a,
\]

\[
 (D_j\tau_{ij})(x)=\frac{\tau_{ij}(x)-\tau_{ij}(x-e_j)}a
 \quad(j\ne i),
\]

the following identities hold as polynomials in independent canonical data on
`Z^3`:

\[
 \dot\rho_x+\sum_i\frac{j_i(x)-j_i(x-e_i)}a=0,
\tag{R8.4}
\]

\[
 \dot j_i(x)+\frac{\tau_{ii}(x+e_i)-\tau_{ii}(x)}a
 +\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\tau_{ij}(x)-\tau_{ij}(x-e_j)}a=0.
\tag{R8.5}
\]

### Energy identity

For each direction `i`, combine the `pi_x nabla^-_i G_i(x)` term coming from
`pi_x dot(pi_x)` with the time derivative of the two adjacent gradient
squares in `rho`.  The result is

\[
 \frac{G_i(x)(\pi_x+\pi_{x+e_i})
       -G_i(x-e_i)(\pi_{x-e_i}+\pi_x)}{2a}
 =-\frac{j_i(x)-j_i(x-e_i)}a.
\]

The mass terms cancel locally.  Summing the three directional identities
proves (R8.4).

### Momentum identity: kinetic, mass, and longitudinal direction

Differentiate the fixed current:

\[
 \dot j_i(x)=-\frac12\bigl(F_x+F_{x+e_i}\bigr)G_i(x)
 -\frac{\pi_{x+e_i}^2-\pi_x^2}{2a},
\quad F_x:=\dot\pi_x.
\tag{R8.6}
\]

The forward difference of `pi^2/2` in (R8.1) cancels the second term of
(R8.6).  The forward difference of `-m^2 phi^2/2` cancels the mass part of
the first term.  Writing `p=G_i(x)`, the longitudinal product gives

\[
 \frac{G_i(x+e_i)G_i(x)-G_i(x)G_i(x-e_i)}{2a}
 =\frac p2\left(\nabla_i^-G_i(x+e_i)+\nabla_i^-G_i(x)\right),
\]

which cancels the longitudinal force contribution in (R8.6).

### Momentum identity: each transverse direction

Fix `ell != i` and abbreviate

\[
 p=G_i(x),\quad
 a_0=G_\ell(x),\quad b=G_\ell(x+e_i),\quad
 c=G_\ell(x-e_\ell),\quad d=G_\ell(x+e_i-e_\ell).
\]

Commutation of forward differences (the exact plaquette curl identity) gives

\[
 G_i(x+e_\ell)=p+b-a_0,\qquad
 G_i(x-e_\ell)=p+c-d.
\]

Substitution in (R8.2) yields

\[
 \frac{\tau_{i\ell}(x)-\tau_{i\ell}(x-e_\ell)}a
 =\frac{p(a_0+b-c-d)}{2a}
 +\frac{b^2-a_0^2-c^2+d^2}{4a}.
\tag{R8.7}
\]

The `ell`-transverse part of the diagonal stress contributes

\[
 \frac{-(b^2+d^2)+(a_0^2+c^2)}{4a}.
\tag{R8.8}
\]

The square remainder in (R8.7) cancels (R8.8) exactly, leaving

\[
 \frac p2\left(\nabla_\ell^-G_\ell(x)
 +\nabla_\ell^-G_\ell(x+e_i)\right),
\]

which cancels the `ell` part of the force in (R8.6).  Doing this for both
transverse directions proves (R8.5).  This is the finite-difference
product-rule cancellation that the one-dimensional calculation could not
display.

## Reciprocal-axis gluing

For component mask `m`, use the physical-position Fourier transform
`F_m(K) = sum_x exp[-i a K dot (x+m/2)] F_m(x)`.
Since all base coordinates `x` are integers, `K_j -> K_j+2pi/a` gives
`F_m -> (-1)^(m_j) F_m`. The checker obtains each bilinear vertex from the
actual real-space rho/j/tau polynomial using two independent plane waves;
it verifies every reciprocal-axis transition, not just a separate mask table.
No inverse lattice derivative, TT projector, or momentum denominator is introduced.

## Sharp negative controls

If the longitudinal term in (R8.1) is replaced by the continuum-looking
average of squares,

\[
 \frac14\left(G_i(x)^2+G_i(x-e_i)^2\right),
\]

then

\[
 \tau_{ii}^{\rm naive}-\tau_{ii}
 =\frac14\left(G_i(x)-G_i(x-e_i)\right)^2,
\]

and the momentum residual is its nonzero forward difference divided by `a`.
Thus the Round-7 one-dimensional mutant remains embedded in every axis of the
3D theory.  A second mutant that averages only one factor in (R8.2) violates
both `tau_ij=tau_ji` and the momentum Ward identity.  The checker requires
both mutants to fail on all three axes.

## Periodic zero modes

On every finite periodic cubic lattice, summing (R8.4) and (R8.5) gives

\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\sum_x\rho_x=0,\qquad
 \frac{d}{dt}P_i=0,
\]

with

\[
 P_i:=\sum_xj_i(x)
 =-\sum_x\pi_x\frac{\phi_{x+e_i}-\phi_{x-e_i}}{2a}.
\tag{R8.9}
\]

Every periodic stress divergence has zero homogeneous component.  A spatially
constant scalar configuration has zero `P_i` but can have nonzero total
energy.  Therefore this matter construction **does not** repair the distinct
Round-7 obstruction that the deleted homogeneous gravity block cannot receive
nonzero total matter energy.  It proves conservation of that zero mode, not a
gravitational receiver for it.

## Exact quantum/Weyl statement

Take `m^2 >= 0` for the stable free Hamiltonian; the polynomial identities also
hold algebraically for real mass-squared. Use the common invariant Schwartz
core in the finite-volume Schrödinger representation for all unbounded
quadratic operators and commutators below. No finite-dimensional matrix
representation of the CCR is claimed.

On a finite periodic lattice impose

\[
 [\widehat\phi_x,\widehat\pi_y]=i\hbar\delta_{xy},\qquad
 [\widehat\phi_x,\widehat\phi_y]
 =[\widehat\pi_x,\widehat\pi_y]=0,
\]

and Weyl-quantize the displayed quadratic polynomials.  For a quadratic
Hamiltonian the Moyal series terminates and

\[
 \frac{i}{\hbar}[\widehat H_m,\widehat F]
 =\widehat{\{F,H_m\}}
\tag{R8.10}
\]

exactly.  In particular, (R8.4) and (R8.5) lift to exact equal-time operator
identities with hats on every composite.  There is no omitted `hbar^2`
remainder or central term.  The two linear factors in the current even commute
directly:

\[
 [\widehat\pi_x+\widehat\pi_{x+e_i},
   \widehat\phi_{x+e_i}-\widehat\phi_x]=0.
\]

This operator statement is finite-volume and free-matter only.  It does not
evaluate a matter-gravity Kubo correlator and does not address the order-`g^2`
constraint brackets identified in Round 7.
