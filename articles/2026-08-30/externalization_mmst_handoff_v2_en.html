4 · Per-item briefs of what is proved

Each brief is intended to be used without the rest of the corpus. Citations are to the attached memorandum unless named otherwise.

4.1 · L3 — crossed product commutes with the limit  [E]

Let G be finite and A1→A2→… an inductive system of unital C*-algebras with injective maps, carrying equivariant actions α(N):G↷AN. Then

varinjlim (AN ⧹α(N) G)  ≅  A ⧹α G

is a *-isomorphism. The proof uses amenability of finite groups (full and reduced crossed products coincide) and that every algebraic crossed-product element lives at one finite stage. The coefficient expectation F(∑g agug)=ae and the group average E(a)=|G|−1∑g αg(a) descend to the limit, are faithful, and obey the uniform Pimsner–Popa bound E(x)≥|G|−1x for x≥0. For G=Z4 the homogeneous subspaces satisfy the fusion Bk Bℓ⊂Bk+ℓ, so Ψλ4 is neutral and Ψλ2 has grade [v].

Index equality with |G| requires outerness of the limiting local action, which is the separate theorem of §4.5, not a formal consequence of the crossed-product exchange. Compatible invariant states satisfy φN∘EN=φN and pass to the limit.

4.2 · UGF — uniform Green-form domination  [E]

For the edge-projected frozen collar, the mode-wise bound

0 ≤ gN(m) ≤ (π2/4) · 1/(2|m|)

follows from the exact Hardy particle–hole count SN(m)≤|m| and the sharp fundamental-domain sine inequality ωN(m)≥(2/π)|m|. Summing eight independent components yields 0≤QN(q) ≤KG Qch(q) with KG=π2/4, independently of N. Pointwise gN(m)→1/(2|m|) plus the uniform bound give local H2 quadratic-form convergence.

The continuum field Y(eλ,f) is a weight-one affine current, hence has a linear energy bound. Transferring the Gaussian contraction through UGF gives the lattice bound (L1) with p=1 and Cf≤C(π2/4,λ) ||f||H2, independent of N, assuming only that the cocycles and zero-mode charge shifts have norm one in all four grades.

Scope. This is an all-N theorem for the edge-projected bosonization. It does not prove that every microscopic full-CAR density belongs to that bosonic edge algebra — that is ALG-EXH. A completely gapless transverse covariance would contribute O(N) particle–hole channels already at m=1 and would violate the bound; the isolated one-branch hypothesis is load-bearing. A gapless transverse mutant, audited numerically, grows from 64.829 to 384.137 across the exhaustion. Measured max 2|m|gN(m)=1.029399027646.

4.3 · TEL-a — dyadic graph defect of rate N−2  [E]

The isometries VN,2N of §2 are explicit. The vertex-multiplier defect satisfies

|rN(m) − 1| ≤ A0 m2/N2,    A0 = 4096(1+π/2),
|r2N(m) − rN(m)| ≤ ATEL m2/N2,    ATEL = 5120(1+π/2).

A Lipschitz estimate for Wick exponentials of the norm-two charge, with undifferentiated contractions dominated by UGF, converts this into the graph-norm bound, for every f∈C∞(S1),

||(Ψλ,2N(f) VN,2N − VN,2N Ψλ,N(f)) (1+L0,N)−3||
+ (adjoint with f̄)  ≤  2 Cλ ATEL ||f||H4 N−2.

Thus TEL-a holds with p=3 and ε=1. The dyadic telescoping series is then geometric of ratio 1/4, so the transported fields and adjoints converge on Dfin. Finite audit: Gram and compatibility errors are exactly zero; measured coefficient envelope max N2|r2N(m)−rN(m)|/m2 =3.420384012310; smooth-test Wick seminorm fits N−2.022861.

Scope. The Shannon embedding is exact in momentum and is not sharply support-preserving in position. TEL-a therefore proves graph convergence of the edge-projected fields, not by itself convergence of sharply localized microscopic algebras. That support/locality statement is ALG-EXH.

4.4 · Relative locality  [E]

For every neutral root α one has ⟨λ,α⟩∈Z. On the simple-root basis of D5⊕A3 the eight pairings are [0,0,0,0,1,1,0,0]; the full root lattice follows by linearity. Finite vertex exchange phases are therefore 1, so

[Ψλ,N(f), Wα,N(g)] ξ = 0

on the finite-energy core whenever f and g have disjoint supports. Transport by VN,2N, TEL-a graph convergence (including adjoints), and Osborne–Stottmeister strong convergence of the bounded neutral Weyl operators pass the identity to the limit. Closability extends it from the core. The integer spin hλ=1 then makes the order-four Q-system commutative, hence the extension local.

4.5 · Z4 outerness in the limit  [E]

On every limiting local factor generated by the neutral algebra and the four charged grades, the canonical endomorphism of the inclusion is multiplicity-free,

ῑ ι  ≃  id ⊕ ρλ ⊕ ρ2λ ⊕ ρ3λ,

with four inequivalent sectors, only the first being the vacuum. Hence A(I)′∩Bgen(I)=C. A unitary in Bgen(I) implementing a non-identity grade automorphism would have to lie in that relative commutant, hence be scalar, contradicting the nontrivial character on Ψλ. Therefore every non-identity element of the Z4 gauge action is outer.

Finite-inner correction, stated so that it is not used as a witness. Every automorphism of M256 is inner. The diagonal unitary U=diag(164, i164, −164, −i164) is charge-zero and implements the finite clock action. Outerness is a local-factor limit statement, not a finite-matrix statement. L3 remains valid in either case; index equality and Q-system irreducibility use the limit outerness.

5 · Remaining item (TEL-B): the matrix-valued two-edge estimate

The exact Hardy model of a quarter twist on two chiral edges has Hilbert–Schmidt divergence ||P0−P1/4||HS2 =(2/π2)log N+O(1). On the frozen QWZ collar the analogous difference of covariances is the matrix identity

CN(0) − CN(1) = DN,top(1/4) ⊕ DN,bottom(1/4) + RN.

The task is to prove CR:=supN≥16||RN||HS<∞ with an explicit constant, and to convert that bound into graph-norm control of the difference between the microscopic charged Bogoliubov implementer and the edge-mode implementer of §4.3. The target value 2.11 is optional for convergence; any finite CR suffices for the Shale-class statement.

What is measured, and what it is not. On N∈{16,24,32,48,64,96} one has max ||RN||HS=2.105079358<2.11, with 1/N extrapolated plateau 2.107565497 and maximum fit residual 2.349×10−5. These numbers support boundedness. They do not prove it for all N. Replacing the universal quantifier by the N≤96 plateau is mathematically invalid.

A sufficient analytic criterion, if a better subtraction is found. Let C(p) be the 16×16 frozen QWZ Fermi symbol of the strip Hamiltonian, and let Pt, Pb be the rank-one boundary projections at p=0. For a matrix-valued regularizer B(p) whose Hankel corners reproduce RN, Peller’s B1/22,2 criterion gives ||HN(B)||HS2 ≤∑k≠0|k| ||Bk||F2, and four corners/orientations produce the safe factor sixteen after squaring. An all-N bound follows from an analytic enclosure of that Besov seminorm. The constant-profile choice of B does not work; see Section 8.

UGF cannot substitute for TEL-B: it controls the scalar edge branch, whereas the remainder is a matrix-valued transverse cancellation.

6 · Remaining item (ALG-EXH): Buchholz–Verch exhaustion

Let Bgen(I) denote the scaling subalgebra generated by the neutral Weyl operators and the four graph-convergent charged grades. Equality with the full microscopic Buchholz–Verch scaling algebra of the collar follows if the following two estimates hold (memorandum (5.26)):

(i) Phase-space tightness. For every β>0,

limE→∞ supN≥16 supx∈BN(I), ||x||≤1 ||(1 − PgenE,N) e−β L0,N x ΩN|| = 0.

(ii) Finite-energy approximation, and exclusion of ultraviolet cluster points. For every finite E and every norm-bounded, translation-continuous local scaling sequence (xN),

limN→∞ infyN∈BNgen(I) ( ||(xN − yN) PE,N|| + ||(xN* − yN*) PE,N|| ) = 0.

Line (i) says that, after Euclidean energy damping, vectors created by the microscopic unit ball are uniformly approximated by the finite-energy neutral-plus-four-grade subspace. Line (ii) says that no bounded operator sequence has additional matrix elements on any finite-energy sector, for the operator and its adjoint. Together they give equality of strong lower limits on the common finite-energy core, and exclude a weak cluster point supported only at energies escaping to infinity. This is the standard scaling-algebra compactness mechanism of Buchholz–Verch, written as the two concrete estimates the collar must satisfy.

What is already in hand, and what cannot substitute. The frozen lattice system has isotony, exact finite-range locality, uniform norm bounds for scaling families, discrete translation covariance, and a translation-covariant neutral scaling limit. UGF, TEL-a and relative locality add uniform charged energy control, graph convergence, and the lower inclusion Bgen(I)⊂B(I). What is not proved is the uniform microscopic phase-space estimate and the upper-inclusion line. No arithmetic, finite sector census, vacuum-character match, or N≤96 remainder plateau can establish them.

An equivalent geometric form of the same residue is a support-preserving microscopic realization of the dyadic embeddings VN,2N (the Shannon embeddings of TEL-a are not position-local).

8 · What we tried in-house (TEL-B), so that it is not repeated

Two distinct attempts on TEL-B were made and stopped. The item is now typed TEL-B-EXTERNAL. No all-N constant is claimed. The useful external contribution is a third angle: a matrix Hardy subtraction without the Brillouin-seam jump, plus conversion to charged-implementer graph convergence.

Angle 1 — finite-exhaustion Hilbert–Schmidt remainder.

The matrix identity ΔN=Dtop⊕Dbottom+RN was computed on the frozen exhaustion, using the exact Hardy kernels for the two opposite-chirality edges together with the Ny=8 transverse zero-energy profiles. Result: max ||RN||HS=2.105079358, 1/N plateau 2.107565497, residual 2.349×10−5. The exact Hardy logarithm (2/π2)log N is thereby subtracted, and the remainder appears to sit. This does not prove supN||RN||HS≤2.11, nor any other all-N constant. A finite logarithmic fit does not prove an asymptotic law, and a six-point plateau does not prove a universal bound. That promotion is the statement the memorandum explicitly refuses.

Angle 2 — constant-profile Hardy subtraction and Peller’s Hankel criterion.

Subtract from the 16×16 Fermi symbol the rank-one p=0 edge profiles, occupied for p>0 (top, slope −1) and p<0 (bottom, slope +1). The remainder B(p) has no infrared jump at p=0. If its B1/22,2 seminorm were bounded by a usable constant, Peller would give TEL-B. On a 4096-point symbol grid the companion computation finds

∑k≠0 |k| ||Bk||F2 = 4.482956087,
4 ||B||Ḣ1/2 = 8.469196974 > 3.

The same grid gives a raw (unsubtracted) seminorm 2.570232705: the constant-profile subtraction increases the Besov norm, because it cancels the infrared jump and exposes a Brillouin-zone-edge ultraviolet discontinuity. Neither a CR=2.5 nor a CR=3 proof follows, and the 8.47 figure is a finite-grid evaluation, not an all-mode enclosure. The unregularized symbol, which retains the edge step, is likewise not a proof: its truncated H1/2 seminorm grows with Fourier resolution. UGF cannot repair the matrix cancellation. This was the second distinct attempt; the stop rule applies.

What a third angle needs. A matrix-valued Hardy (or other symbol) subtraction whose jump is supported only at the Fermi points and is smooth at the Brillouin seams ±π, with an analytic enclosure of the resulting B1/22,2 (or equivalent Schatten) norm, together with a comparison of the associated Bogoliubov implementer to the edge-mode Fock implementer of TEL-a in graph norm on Dfin. Optimizing the failed constant-profile symbol, or re-fitting the six-point HS plateau, is not that angle.
